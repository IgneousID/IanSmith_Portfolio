01 · Base camp

About me

IS

Hi, I’m Ian Smith. I’m a product strategist and designer, finishing my BFA in UX Design at the University of North Texas.

I’ve never thought of myself as an artist, but I’ve always been a systems thinker. I started out in engineering and moved into design because it offered the same problem solving with far more breadth. Product strategy is where it all clicks for me: thinking in systems, big and small, and figuring out what’s worth building.

How I work

I start with context: where, why and how. As I learn more, I move into abstraction, boiling a problem down to its simplest parts and why each one matters to the story. I think out loud and on a whiteboard, and I’m driven to make things happen.

I’m also working to become a better storyteller. I’ve joined my school’s improv club and my local Toastmasters chapter, so I can explain design decisions with confidence and bring stakeholders along with a clear narrative.

Where I’ve been

  • DFW Airport Innovation project manager intern, 2026
  • EndoMD Lead web developer and research assistant, 2024 to now
  • Target Design researcher, student research contract, 2024 to 2025
  • Citi Analyst and UI developer intern, 2023

Off the clock

You’ll usually find me hiking, camping or on a road trip. I’m working toward visiting every Texas state park. I love hockey, and I’ve always been a maker.

02 · Summit

Innovation Consumer Council

Consumer feedback in 10 days, not 2 months.

Role
Innovation Project Manager (intern), project lead
Timeline
May – Jul 2026 (10 weeks)
Team
Led it myself, with oversight from my manager and support from a 5-person innovation team

I stood up DFW Airport's Innovation Consumer Council, a fast way for the innovation team to hear from real travelers before committing money to an idea. Its first study went from concept selection to 240 consumer responses and a leadership readout in about 10 days. The traditional route took about 2 months and could cost $20,000 or more.

Where it got hard

  • I lost half my internship before the real work could start.
  • Recruiting the right people, fast.
  • Owning it alone.

03 · The falls

Cooperative Connections

Helping rural electric co-ops mobilize the help their communities already have.

Role
Team lead & project manager
Timeline
Fall 2026 capstone, UNT
Team
2 (with Quincy)

When the power goes out in rural Texas, help is often far away, but neighbors, churches and local groups already step in. Cooperative Connections is a dashboard and partner portal that gives rural electric cooperatives a map of that community help. It shows where coverage has gaps and helps them fill those gaps with local partners, so outages hurt less without building anything new.

Where it got hard

  • The system was enormous.
  • Our first concept was too vague.
  • Leading a two-person capstone at full scale.

04 · The lake

HeatWise

Personal heat risk for people in cities that were never built for heat.

Role
Team lead & project manager
Timeline
Fall 2026 capstone, UNT
Team
2 (with Gabi)

Heat waves are reaching U.S. cities that have little experience managing them. HeatWise is a mobile app for renters, older adults and people with health conditions. It explains what today's heat means for their body, shows where they can cool down right now, and helps them check on the people they care for.

Where it got hard

  • Our scope was too big, and it was costing us.
  • Choosing between two good directions.
  • Keeping a two-person team moving.
