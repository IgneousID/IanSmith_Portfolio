Innovation Consumer Council

DFW Airport

Consumer feedback in 10 days, not 2 months.

Role
Innovation Project Manager (intern), project lead
Timeline
May – Jul 2026 (10 weeks)
Team
Led it myself, with oversight from my manager and support from a 5-person innovation team
Tools
Remesh, PowerPoint, vendor research panel

In short

I stood up DFW Airport's Innovation Consumer Council, a fast way for the innovation team to hear from real travelers before committing money to an idea. Its first study went from concept selection to 240 consumer responses and a leadership readout in about 10 days. The traditional route took about 2 months and could cost $20,000 or more.

Where it got hard

The problem

DFW's Innovation division explores new ventures for one of the busiest airports in the world. The catch was that ideas often moved forward, and picked up cost, before anyone knew whether travelers actually wanted them. The airport's Consumer Insights studies were rigorous, but at roughly 2 months and $20K+ each, they were built for big decisions, not for checking an early idea.

The innovation team needed something in between: less precise than a full study, much faster, and close enough to tell a promising concept from a weak one.

The question I ownedHow might DFW's innovation team get credible consumer feedback on an early concept in days instead of months?

Constraints

  • Time. I had 10 weeks, and the vendor relationship meant hands-on project work couldn't start until week 5. Everything that mattered had to happen in 6 weeks.
  • Many stakeholders. For the council to last beyond my internship, it had to be useful to other business units, not just Innovation.
  • No precedent. There was no existing process, discussion guide or audience definition to start from.

Where it got hard

Struggle 1 of 3

I lost half my internship before the real work could start.

Because of how the vendor relationship had to be set up, hands-on research couldn't begin until week 5 of 10. For a program meant to prove speed, that was a real risk. I could have ended the summer with nothing to show.

How I got through it

I stopped waiting on the contract and used those weeks for the work that didn't need the vendor. I ran a test session on Remesh with internal stakeholders and used it to shape the council around what other business units needed. So the day the vendor was ready, we already knew what to test and who to ask. The whole program then ran in 6 weeks.

Struggle 2 of 3

Recruiting the right people, fast.

A 10-day turnaround only works if you can reach the right travelers quickly, and recruitment was the other half of my time crunch.

How I got through it

We reached 240 participants.

Struggle 3 of 3

Owning it alone.

I worked largely autonomously, and the council only works if business units I had no authority over decide to use it.

How I got through it

I treated them as customers. I listened first, stayed their go-to contact all summer, and framed the council around their problems instead of Innovation's.

What I did, and why

1. Set up the vendor partnership. Why: Without a research partner there was no panel and no speed. I negotiated the contract with a research vendor and became their main point of contact. I compressed onboarding so recruiting and fielding could start the moment the contract was in place.

2. Asked the business what it needed. Why: A council that only Innovation uses dies when its champion leaves. Before testing anything with consumers, I ran a session on Remesh with internal stakeholders from other business units. The goal was to learn where a fast feedback loop would help them most, and to make the council something they'd want to use rather than something Innovation owned alone. I stayed their liaison throughout.

3. Built the first study end to end. Why: The fastest way to prove the model was to run a real study through it, not write a proposal about it.

  • Selected a concept to test
  • Defined the target audience
  • Wrote the discussion guide
  • Made a short stimulus video to help participants picture the concept
  • Recruited 240 participants for an asynchronous Remesh session that collected both quantitative ratings and open-ended responses

4. Read it out to leadership. Why: Research only matters if it changes a decision. I synthesized the 240 responses into a readout for leadership: what travelers liked, what worried them, and what would have to be true for the concept to succeed.

What we learned

Travelers found the concept appealing but raised specific concerns. That's a useful result. Instead of a vague "people like it," leadership got a concrete list of what to solve and measure against if they move forward.

Outcome

  • About 10 days from concept selection to results, versus roughly 2 months for a traditional study
  • 240 participants, with quantitative and qualitative data
  • A go/no-go framework: clear criteria leadership can use to decide whether, and how, to pursue the concept
  • A repeatable process (vendor, audience, guide template and readout format) that other business units can bring ideas to

Reflection

The hardest part wasn't the research. It was the relationships. The council only matters if business units trust it enough to bring it their ideas, so I spent as much time listening to them as I did designing the study. I also learned to treat "fast and close enough" as a deliberate product decision, not a compromise. Knowing what level of confidence a decision actually needs is half of product strategy.

If I did it again, I'd set up a lightweight way to track which concepts the council touched and what happened to them, so its value is easy to show over time.
